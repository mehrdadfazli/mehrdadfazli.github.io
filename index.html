Mehrdad Fazli

Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science · George Mason University

I am a Ph.D. candidate at George Mason University, advised by Dr. Ziwei Zhu. My research focuses on hallucination in large vision-language models (LVLMs) — understanding why multimodal models describe things that aren't there, and designing methods to make them more faithful. I also work on the robustness of medical AI and on explainability of LLM decisions.

I have interned as a Ph.D. Software Engineer at Google (Google Cloud), an Applied Scientist at Amazon, and a Data Science & ML Intern at Wayfair. Before GMU, I earned an M.Sc. in Systems Engineering from the University of Virginia, where I applied machine learning to human activity recognition and COVID-19 forecasting.

I expect to graduate in May 2027.

Research Projects

Multimodal AI · Current

Hallucination Analysis & Mitigation in LVLMs

Confidence-Aware Attention Calibration (CAAC) reduces object hallucination through visual-token calibration and adaptive attention re-scaling, evaluated on CHAIR, AMBER, and POPE. Follow-up work injects context embeddings to heal hallucinations and rethinks how faithfulness is evaluated.

arXiv Code
Medical AI · Robustness

Stress-Testing Medical AI (VeriSim)

A configurable framework that simulates realistic patient communication noise to test how reliably medical AI systems hold up outside clean benchmark conditions.

EMNLP 2026
Explainable AI

Prototype-Based Explanations for LLMs

A prototype-based framework that makes LLM classification decisions interpretable by grounding predictions in representative examples.

AAAI 2026
Recommender Systems · Ongoing

Polarization & Filter Bubbles in Recommendation

A theoretical study of echo chambers when content creators adapt to their audiences, showing that extreme item-side fairness constraints can increase user polarization.

Time Series · Epidemiology

COVID-19 Forecasting with Deep Learning

Temporal convolutional networks and Temporal Fusion Transformers using wastewater viral load and socio-economic data to improve COVID-19 forecasts.
